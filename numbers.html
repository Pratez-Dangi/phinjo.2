COORDINATES & Y-LEVELS

Use these as common guideposts in modern Minecraft. Terrain and ore generation can vary.

Reference Coordinate / amount Why it matters
Surface example Y 63 A familiar surface-height reference, not a fixed world surface.
Iron reference Y 16 A useful level for iron in many ordinary Overworld areas; iron also generates at other elevations.
Deepslate transition Near Y 0 Deepslate becomes more common as you descend through the transition zone.
Diamond reference Y -59 A popular depth for diamond mining in modern worlds; watch for lava and caves.

Depth cheat sheet

Lower is not always safer.

Diamond becomes more common at lower levels, but lava, large cave systems, and falls can turn a mining trip into a rescue mission. Bring light, food, and blocks for a safe route.

Coordinates: In Java Edition, enable coordinates through the appropriate debug display/settings. In Bedrock Edition, enable “Show Coordinates” in world settings where available.

Crafting targets

HOW MUCH SHOULD I BRING?

Practical starting amounts, not strict requirements. Your world's portal generation and play style may change what you need.

10 obsidianMinimum for a standard cornerless Nether portal frame.
12 EyesMaximum portal sockets to fill, though some can generate pre-filled.
12–16 EyesA useful target for travelling and activating a portal, with a buffer for breakage.
2+ pickaxesBring a spare for long mining trips; exact needs depend on durability and gear.
Food stackCarry enough to recover health and handle detours. Longer trips need more.
Extra blocksUseful for bridges, cover, quick pillars, and making routes back obvious.
